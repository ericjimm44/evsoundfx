Running it on your Tesla

  1. Park (or have a passenger drive) and open the Tesla web browser — the app icon in the launcher, then this page's URL.
  2. Tap Tap to Start, then allow location when asked so sound reacts to real speed.
  3. Turn on Use GPS speed. Set volume. The Tesla plays browser audio through the car speakers.
  4. Keep this browser tab in the foreground while driving — audio and GPS pause if it's fully backgrounded.
  5. Exterior boost maxes loudness for a Bluetooth speaker mounted outside.

Playing it OUTSIDE the car

The Tesla browser can only use the cabin speakers — so for exterior sound, use your phone as the engine:

  1. Get a loud, weather-resistant Bluetooth speaker and mount it low on the car — inside the frunk near the vents, or secured under the rear bumper (zip ties + a dry spot).
  2. Open this app on your phone, pair the phone to the speaker, and keep the phone in the car with the screen on (the app keeps it awake).
  3. Turn on Use GPS speed (phone GPS drives the revs) and Exterior boost — boost trims the deep bass small speakers can't play and pushes loudness hard.

Exterior sound rules vary by state/city — keep it reasonable in traffic and save the full send for meets, car shows, and private roads.

Want real recorded engine sounds?

The built-in voices are synthesized. For the real thing, add recorded engine loops — they pitch-follow your speed automatically:

  1. Quick (this device): tap the ➕ Add sound chip and pick an MP3/WAV engine loop. It's stored on-device and appears as a new voice.
  2. Permanent (every device): put loop files in the repo's samples/ folder and list them in samples/pack.json. Multiple loops recorded at different RPMs crossfade for extra realism.

Free, legal sources: freesound.org (filter license "Creative Commons 0"), pixabay.com/sound-effects, the BBC Sound Effects archive (personal use). Search for "V8 engine loop", "engine idle loop", "engine rpm steady". Short seamless loops (1–10 s, constant rpm) work best.
